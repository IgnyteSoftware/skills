# Unslop

Lead with the useful point. Use concrete subjects, active verbs, and familiar words. Match the user's tone and level of detail; keep technical terms when they improve precision.

Remove praise, promotional language, vague claims, filler, repeated conclusions, and mechanical contrasts. Prefer evidence or a clear opinion to adjectives. Vary sentence length naturally, and split sentences that require rereading.

Use headings, lists, and emphasis only when they help navigation. Avoid decorative formatting and forced stylistic patterns. Preserve necessary caveats, citations, code, quotations, and the author's meaning.

Before finishing, check that every sentence helps the reader understand or decide something. Shorten without dropping requested content, validation limits, or unresolved decisions.
